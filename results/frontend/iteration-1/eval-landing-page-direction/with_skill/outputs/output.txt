For an indie note-taking app for writers, I’d avoid the usual “AI productivity dashboard” aesthetic and design it around the feeling writers are actually chasing: **a quiet room, a blank page, and the moment an idea starts moving.**

## Design direction: “The Writer’s Studio”

### Core idea
The landing page should feel less like software marketing and more like entering a personal writing space. The product is a tool, but the emotional promise is **focus, ownership, and craft**.

The visual reference points would be:
- a well-used notebook on a wooden desk
- a novelist’s manuscript margin notes
- a minimalist publishing studio
- a late-night writing session with a warm lamp

Not “beige AI startup.” More tactile, more literary.

---

## Visual system

### Palette

A restrained palette built around paper, ink, and physical materials:

- **Ivory paper** — `#F5F0E6`  
  The canvas background, softer than pure white.

- **Charcoal ink** — `#191816`  
  Primary text, strong but not harsh.

- **Faded graphite** — `#6E6860`  
  Secondary text and metadata.

- **Oxidized copper** — `#B56A3B`  
  A single accent for buttons, highlights, and active states.

- **Deep moss** — `#344238`  
  Used sparingly for a sense of calm and depth.

The risk: make it feel like an actual writing environment rather than a SaaS template.

---

## Typography

Typography should carry the personality.

**Display face:**
- ["brand","Fraunces","variable serif typeface"] or another expressive serif  
  Used only for the hero and major statements.

Large, slightly imperfect-feeling headlines:
> “Your thoughts deserve a quieter place.”

**Body:**
- ["brand","Inter","sans-serif typeface"] or a similarly neutral humanist sans  
  Keeps the interface explanations readable.

**Utility text:**
- A small mono font for details like word counts, dates, revision history:
  ```
  Draft 047 · 2,381 words · Edited 8 min ago
  ```

The contrast between literary serif and practical utility type reinforces “writer + tool.”

## Signature element

The memorable detail: **a living manuscript margin.**

Instead of standard feature cards, the page occasionally shows handwritten-style annotations appearing beside product screenshots:

> “Find this idea later”  
> “Character notes”  
> “Rewrite this paragraph”

The annotations connect the digital tool to the physical habits writers already have.

It gives the page a recognizable identity without turning it into a scrapbook.

---

## Sections

### 1. Hero — “The first page”
A large writing canvas immediately visible. The visitor understands the product within seconds.

### 2. Problem — “Writing gets scattered”
Show fragments:
- notes app snippets
- browser tabs
- paper scraps
- half-finished drafts

Then show the app bringing them together.

### 3. Features — “Tools that disappear”
Avoid feature grids. Writers don’t buy “AI-powered organization.” They buy fewer interruptions.

Use editorial-style sections:

**Capture**
> Catch the sentence before it disappears.

**Shape**
> Turn fragments into outlines.

**Return**
> Always find where you left off.

### 4. Community / trust
Show excerpts from fictional writers’ workflows rather than generic testimonials.

Example:
```
Maya — novelist

Drafting:
43 notes
7 scenes
2 possible endings
```

---

## Motion

Keep animation rare and intentional:

- A cursor blinking in the hero document
- Notes gently rearranging into a structure
- A page turning transition between sections

Avoid floating blobs, gradient animations, and excessive parallax. Those make creative tools feel like every other startup.

---

## One deliberate aesthetic risk

I’d make the app screenshot slightly imperfect:
- visible cursor position
- realistic unfinished sentences
- messy notes
- crossed-out ideas

Most software pages show polished empty states. For writers, the magic is in the mess before the finished work.

The page’s message becomes:

**“This is not where your perfect writing appears. This is where it begins.”**
